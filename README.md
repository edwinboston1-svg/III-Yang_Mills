# III — Yang–Mills

**The Ground: Yang–Mills Theory after the Alpöge–Sussman Manuscripts.** Edwin Boston III, 28 October 2026; revised 29 October 2026 (revision 11).

A proof that quantum Yang–Mills theory has a mass gap must stand on a construction of the theory. *The Ground* fixes that ground. It reads the public corpus of Levent Alpöge and Ethan Sussman, which carries Bałaban's renormalisation group from the lattice to the continuum for SU(2), from its primary sources, and proves clause by clause what the corpus asserts and at what strength: in three dimensions, Osterwalder–Schrader Schwinger functions of the plaquette-energy density in the clustering components of subsequential infinite-volume limits, with a short-distance law and no mass gap; in four dimensions, a conditional deduction resting on declared inputs, two of its supporting statements not yet written. It then proves that for a reflection-positive theory a mass gap is the same property as exponential clustering of the reflection-positive correlations, and that a lower bound on the lattice mass gap, uniform in physical units along the construction's own sequence of lattices, supplies the three properties the corpus withholds: a unique vacuum, the cluster property and a mass gap. It then reduces that inequality to two finite statements (Section 8.3): if, at a fixed physical scale, the blocked measure satisfies Dobrushin's condition with an exponential weight and the field inside the blocks clusters at the block scale, both uniformly in the cutoff, then every limit point has a unique vacuum and a mass gap of at least min(κ, log(1/ρ′))/a_c (Theorem 8.5), with the tilt lemmas that make the first hypothesis's uniformity in the cutoff exact (Proposition 8.4); the paper says why the hypothesis cannot be stated on the fine measure's blocks. Those two statements are the object on which the proof that follows will stand. Section 6.6 places two further claims in public view beside the corpus, the OpenAI announcement of 26 October 2026 and the published paper of Faizal and Shabir, the latter read at the five places where its argument must carry weight; Section 8 carries, of record, the sharp one-link constant 2β/(3π) with the disks 3π/72 and 3π/56, the certified hierarchical descent and the transform on an exact tube, and states why the block criterion's widening is not yet a theorem.
