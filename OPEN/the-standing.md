## 1. The answer

**The Clay problem for SU(2), reduced.** The Millennium statement asks, for every compact simple gauge group G, for a non-trivial quantum Yang–Mills theory on ℝ⁴ satisfying the Osterwalder–Schrader (or Wightman) axioms, with a mass gap Δ > 0. For G = SU(2) this tree has PROVED (THE GROUND, Theorems 7.1, 7.4 and 8.5) that the mass gap and the uniqueness of the vacuum hold for every limit point, given three statements:

- **(E) A CONSTRUCTION.** Wilson lattice measures on a scaling sequence of spacings a_j = a₀2^{−K_j}, with couplings tuned along it, whose Schwinger functions of gauge-invariant observables converge along a subsequence. The axioms of the limit (Euclidean invariance among them) and its non-triviality are further parts of (E) that the Clay statement needs and the gap theorem does not.
- **(H-I) HYPOTHESIS I** at a fixed physical scale a_c, uniformly in the cutoff: the blocked measure's coarse variables satisfy Dobrushin's condition with an exponential weight, Σ_{y′} C(y, y′)e^{κ|y−y′|} ≤ α < 1 for every coarse point y.
- **(H-II) HYPOTHESIS II** at the same scale: the field inside the blocks clusters at the block scale, for every coarse configuration.

Then every limit point has a unique vacuum and no spectrum in (0, m), with m = (1/a_c)·min(κ, log 1/ρ′).

**None of (E), (H-I), (H-II) is proved in this tree.** (E) is claimed by others and is not verified here (§8). (H-II) is exact for the one-step perfect decimation and OPEN in the form the reduction consumes, as THE GROUND says in its own text (§6). (H-I) is the crossover, the non-perturbative content of the problem. It is OPEN. Its integrand was measured today in its own currency, THE GROUND's (8.5), on the only exact objects that reach the crossover scale (§7).

**At the strong-coupling end the gap is proved.** The lattice gap at strong coupling for SU(2) on ℤ⁴ is PROVED to β = 0.2452 on proved lemmas alone (Theorem 8.7), and CONDITIONAL to β = 0.4764 on one lemma (Theorem 8.9). Nothing above 0.2452 is proved unconditionally, and nothing above 0.4764 is proved at all.

**What that amounts to.** The continuum mass gap has been turned from a statement about a limit into two inequalities on finite lattice blocks at one fixed physical scale, with every step of the turning proved and its finite skeleton checked by the Lean kernel. The strong-coupling side of the needed inequality is certified. The part that remains open is the part no published method reaches: the blocked measure at effective couplings of order one, between the gas's radius and Bałaban's small-field regime, whose threshold β_UV no paper states.
