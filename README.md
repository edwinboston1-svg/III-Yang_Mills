# III — Yang–Mills

**The Ground: Yang–Mills Theory after the Alpöge–Sussman Manuscripts.** Edwin Boston III, 28 October 2026; revised 29 October 2026 (revision 6).

A proof that quantum Yang–Mills theory has a mass gap must stand on a construction of the theory. *The Ground* fixes that ground. It reads the public corpus of Levent Alpöge and Ethan Sussman, which carries Bałaban's renormalisation group from the lattice to the continuum for SU(2), from its primary sources, and proves clause by clause what the corpus asserts and at what strength: in three dimensions, Osterwalder–Schrader Schwinger functions of the plaquette-energy density in the clustering components of subsequential infinite-volume limits, with a short-distance law and no mass gap; in four dimensions, a conditional deduction resting on declared inputs, two of its supporting statements not yet written. It then proves that for a reflection-positive theory a mass gap is the same property as exponential clustering of the reflection-positive correlations, and that a lower bound on the lattice mass gap, uniform in physical units along the construction's own sequence of lattices, supplies the three properties the corpus withholds: a unique vacuum, the cluster property and a mass gap. It then reduces that inequality to one finite statement (Section 8.3): if, at a fixed physical scale, the conditional law of a block of links given all the others depends on them with Dobrushin's total influence below one, uniformly in the cutoff, then every limit point has a unique vacuum and a mass gap of at least log(1/α)/a_c (Theorem 8.4); the same conclusion follows from a bound on the blocked measure together with a bound on the field inside the blocks (Theorem 8.6), with the tilt lemmas that make the bound's uniformity in the cutoff exact (Proposition 8.5). That statement is the object on which the proof that follows will stand.
