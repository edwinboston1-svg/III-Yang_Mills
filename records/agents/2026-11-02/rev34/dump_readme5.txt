[00] A proof that quantum Yang–Mills theory has a mass gap must stand on a construction of the theory.
[01]  *The Ground* fixes that ground.
[02]  It reads the public corpus of Levent Alpöge and Ethan Sussman, which carries Bałaban's renormalisation group from the lattice to the continuum for SU(2), from its primary sources, and proves clause by clause what the corpus asserts and at what strength:
[03]  in three dimensions, Osterwalder–Schrader Schwinger functions of the plaquette-energy density in the clustering components of subsequential infinite-volume limits, with a short-distance law and no mass gap;
[04]  in four dimensions, a conditional deduction resting on declared inputs, two of its supporting statements not yet written.
[05]  It then proves that for a reflection-positive theory a mass gap is the same property as exponential clustering of the reflection-positive correlations, and that a lower bound on the lattice mass gap, uniform in physical units along the construction's own sequence of lattices, supplies the three properties the corpus withholds:
[06]  a unique vacuum, the cluster property and a mass gap.
[07]  It then reduces that inequality to two finite statements (Section 8.3):
[08]  if, at a fixed physical scale, the blocked measure satisfies Dobrushin's condition with an exponential weight and the field inside the blocks clusters at the block scale, both uniformly in the cutoff, then every limit point has a unique vacuum and a mass gap of at least min(κ, log(1/ρ′))/a_c (Theorem 8.5), with the tilt lemmas that make the first hypothesis's uniformity in the cutoff exact (Proposition 8.4);
[09]  the paper says why the hypothesis cannot be stated on the fine measure's blocks.
[10]  Those two statements are the object on which the proof that follows will stand.
[11]  At the strong end the paper proves a theorem (Section 8.3, Theorem 8.7, and on ℤ³ at β ≤ 0.3739, Theorem 8.8):
[12]  for the SU(2) Wilson lattice gauge theory on ℤ⁴ at β ≤ 0.241, an exact decimation of one link in every plaquette, along a perfect cover of period two, leaves a Gibbs measure of finite range whose Dobrushin row sum is at most 0.9963, with constants proved from Turán's inequality for the modified Bessel functions (Lemma 8.6);
[13]  the infinite-volume Gibbs measure is therefore unique, correlations of local observables decay exponentially, and the transfer matrix has a mass gap.
[14]  The numbers of its certificate are evaluated on three roads that share no line, and with the exact constants, enclosed in a gate run of record, the bound extends to β ≤ 0.2452;
[15]  the derivation's test from outside, a search from below over the decimated specification, found no configuration exceeding its constants, and a second reader's independent line-by-line read found no defect.
[16]  The theorem is a statement about the lattice theory at strong coupling.
[17]  On ℤ⁴ the flux-surface gas, which prices the surfaces of the polymer expansion by the representation theory of SU(2), extends it to β ≤ 0.4764, conditional on one stated lemma on contact leaves (Theorem 8.9).
[18]  On the hierarchical model, where the Migdal–Kadanoff recursion is the exact renormalisation group, the paper certifies the crossing of the crossover:
[19]  from β = 2.2 and from β = 2.3 the eighth iterate of the Wilson weight satisfies Dobrushin's condition on ℤ⁴ with row sums at most 0.0336 and 0.2820, every quantity an interval at 256 bits, and the gauge groups part in the rate of descent, SU(2) at the rate of ℤ⁴'s one-loop running and U(1) exponentially slowly (Section 8.3).
[20]  The four-dimensional mass gap of the continuum theory is the object the proof that follows must reach.
[21]  The paper closes Section 8.3 by stating the open part exactly:
[22]  the group enters only through the evaluation of Hypothesis I, the weak end has no published coupling (Bałaban's four-dimensional stability is proved under the hypothesis that every effective coupling of the sequence stays below a constant;
[23]  the discharge of that hypothesis, his Theorem 2, was announced in 1987 and never published, and it is a theorem on this work's path, stated exactly on the open ledger with its falsifier), the distance is [0.2452, 1/γ²] on proved lemmas and [0.4764, 1/γ²] under Theorem 8.9's condition, with γ unread, and the tools that would cross it are named and not claimed.
