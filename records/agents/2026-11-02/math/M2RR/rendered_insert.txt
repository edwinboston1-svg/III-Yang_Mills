The reduction has a second form, in the currency in which the gap itself is an exact quantity. It replaces the supremum of Hypothesis II by an average over the blocked measure and the total variation of (8.5) by a maximal correlation, and its conclusion is that of Theorem 8.5.

Proposition 8.1′ (The gap is an L² norm). *Let T be a transfer operator on a compact space X with kernel T(x, dy) = t(x, y) dy, t continuous, strictly positive and symmetric, and T positive as an operator on L²(dy); let λ₀ > λ₁ be its two largest eigenvalues, ψ₀ the positive eigenfunction normalised in L²(dy), π(dx) = ψ₀(x)² dx, and P the Doob transform of Proposition 8.1. Then P is self-adjoint on L²(π); on L²₀(π), the functions of π-mean zero, ‖Pᵏ‖ = (λ₁/λ₀)ᵏ for every k ≥ 1, so that m**_{lat}** = −log ‖P‖**_{L²₀(π)}**; ‖Pᵏ‖**_{L²₀(π)}** ≤ δ(Pᵏ); and, with the eigenvalues λᵢ of T counted with multiplicity,*

	 $∫ χ²(P^{k}(x, ·) ‖ π) π(dx) = Tr T^{2k}/λ₀^{2k} − 1 = Σ_{i≥1} (λᵢ/λ₀)^{2k}.$ 	(8.8a)

*Proof. *For bounded *u* and *w*, ∫ *w* · *Pu* dπ = λ₀⁻¹ ∬ *w*(*x*)ψ₀(*x*) *t*(*x*, *y*) ψ₀(*y*)*u*(*y*) *dx dy*, which is symmetric in *u* and *w*; so *P* is self-adjoint on L²(π). Multiplication by ψ₀ is a unitary map *U* of L²(π) onto L²(*dx*), ψ₀ being continuous and bounded below on the compact space *X*, and (*T*/λ₀)(ψ₀*u*) = ψ₀ *Pu*, that is *UPU*⁻¹ = *T*/λ₀. The map *U* carries the constants to ψ₀ and L²₀(π) onto the orthogonal complement of ψ₀, so *P* on L²₀(π) is unitarily equivalent to *T*/λ₀ on that complement, a compact positive operator whose norm is its largest eigenvalue λ₁/λ₀; powers pass through the equivalence. For an eigenvalue λ ≠ 0 of *T* with eigenfunction ψ, the eigenfunction ψ/ψ₀ of *P* is continuous, hence bounded, and it is nonconstant when λ ≠ λ₀, so the oscillation argument of Proposition 8.1 gives |λ/λ₀|^{k} ≤ δ(*P*^{k}) for every eigenvalue λ ≠ λ₀ of *T*; the norm of the self-adjoint *P*^{k} on L²₀(π) is the largest of these numbers. Finally *P*^{k}(*x*, *dy*) has density *p*_{k}(*x*, *y*) = *t*_{k}(*x*, *y*)/(λ₀^{k} ψ₀(*x*)ψ₀(*y*)) with respect to π, *t*_{k} the kernel of *T*^{k}, so ∬ *p*_{k}² dπ dπ = λ₀^{−2k} ∬ *t*_{k}(*x*, *y*)² *dx dy* = λ₀^{−2k} Tr *T*^{2k} = Σ_{i} (λ_{i}/λ₀)^{2k}, the operator *T*^{k} being Hilbert–Schmidt with continuous symmetric kernel; since ∫ *p*_{k}(*x*, *y*) π(*dy*) = 1 for every *x*, ∬ (*p*_{k} − 1)² dπ dπ = Tr *T*^{2k}/λ₀^{2k} − 1, and the inner integral is χ²(*P*^{k}(*x*, ·) ‖ π). ∎[fn]

The average in (8.8a) is at least the multiplicity of λ₁ times (λ₁/λ₀)^{2k}, so it controls the gap only once the decay beats that multiplicity; the norm carries no multiplicity, and Proposition 8.1 dominates it by a supremum over pairs of starting points. The reduction below is stated in the same currency.

For the reduction in this currency the blocked measure is assumed to have the form

	 $λ_{j}(dv) = e^{−A_{j}(v)} ∏_{y∈Y} m(dv_{y}),$ 	(S)

with *m* a Borel probability measure on *V*, *Y* the set of coarse points of the lattice, and *A*_{j} a measurable function finite ∏*m*-almost everywhere: the effective action at the coarse scale. A block map is translation covariant when it commutes with the translations of the lattice by multiples of *a*_{c}; its blocked measure is then invariant under the translations of *Y*. For a rung *j* and a lattice of the construction, the inner product of L²(λ_{j}) is ⟨*F*, *G*⟩ = λ_{j}(*F̄G*); in Lemmas 8.5A and 8.5B, Proposition 8.5C, Theorem 8.5D and Proposition 8.5E the rung and the lattice are fixed. For *y* ∈ *Y* let *H*_{y} be the closed subspace of L²(λ_{j}) of functions of *v*_{yᶜ} = (*v*_{z})_{z≠y}, *K*_{y} the orthogonal projection onto it, which is the conditional expectation *K*_{y}*F* = λ_{j}(*F* | *v*_{yᶜ}), and *Q*_{y} = *I* − *K*_{y}. The L² local fluctuation of *F* ∈ L²(λ_{j}) at *y* is

	 $ℓ_{y}(F) = ‖Q_{y}F‖ = λ_{j}(Var_{λ_{j}}(F | v_{yᶜ}))^{1/2};$ 

it vanishes unless *F* depends on *v*_{y}, it equals ℓ_{y}(*F̄*), and for bounded *F* it is at most δ_{y}(*F*)/2, with δ_{y}(*F*) the oscillation of *F* under changes of *v*_{y} alone, a variance being at most a quarter of the squared oscillation. For distinct *y*, *y*′ write ξ = (*v*_{z})_{z≠y,y′} for the coarse environment of the pair, π_{ξ} for a version of the conditional law of (*v*_{y}, *v*_{y′}) given ξ, and

	 $ρ_{ξ}(y, y′) = sup { Cov_{π_{ξ}}(f(v_{y}), g(v_{y′})) : Var_{π_{ξ}} f = Var_{π_{ξ}} g = 1 }$ 

for its maximal correlation, the supremum over real or over complex functions being the same; it is a countable supremum over *f* and *g* in a uniformly dense subset of the continuous functions on *V*, hence measurable in ξ. The conditional maximal correlation matrix of the blocked measure is

	 $R_{j}(y, y′) = ess sup_{ξ} ρ_{ξ}(y, y′),     R_{j}(y, y) = 0,$ 	(8.8b)

the essential supremum taken under λ_{j}. It is symmetric, its entries lie in [0, 1], and it does not depend on the versions.[fn]

Hypothesis I′ (The blocked bound in L²). *There are numbers κ > 0 and α < 1 and a rung j₀ such that at every rung j ≥ j₀, on every lattice of the construction, Σ**_{y′}** R**_{j}**(y, y′) e**^{κ|y−y′|}** ≤ α for every coarse point y.*

Hypothesis II′ (The fibre clustering, averaged over the blocked measure). *There are a number ρ′ < 1, a constant K′ and a rung j₀ such that at every rung j ≥ j₀, on every lattice of the construction, for all bounded observables f and g, with d the distance between S(f) and S(g):*

*(a) |λ**_{j}**( ν**_{j}**(fg | ·) − ν**_{j}**(f | ·) ν**_{j}**(g | ·) )| ≤ K′ N(f) ‖f‖ ‖g‖ ρ′**^{d}**;*

*(b) the fibre mean F = ν**_{j}**(f | ·), as a function of the coarse configuration, satisfies ℓ**_{y}**(F) ≤ K′ N(f) ‖f‖ ρ′**^{r}** for every coarse point y, where r is the distance from y to S(f).*

Both hypotheses involve the fibre measure and the conditional laws of λ_{j} only through λ_{j}-integrals and λ_{j}-essential suprema, so they do not depend on the versions. The one supremum in them is the essential supremum over the coarse environment in (8.8b): the conditional law of a pair of coarse variables is still taken at every environment, but the field inside the blocks enters it integrated with its true conditional weight, and in Hypothesis II′ an adversarial coarse configuration enters weighted by its probability. Neither hypothesis refers to a limit, and neither asks for a relation between rungs: each is used at each rung separately.

Hypothesis II implies Hypothesis II′ with the same ρ′ and *K*′, since the λ_{j}-mean of a function is at most its supremum and ℓ_{y}(*F*) ≤ δ_{y}(*F*)/2. Hypothesis I implies Hypothesis I′ with the same κ and α when the block map is translation covariant, by Proposition 8.5E below; when it is not, Hypothesis I′ follows from Hypothesis I with ½(α + ᾱ) in place of α, provided the weighted column sums Σ_{y′} *C*^{λ}_{j}(*y*′, *y*)*e*^{κ|y−y′|} are at most some ᾱ with α + ᾱ < 2.

Lemma 8.5A (Two projections). *Let P and Q be orthogonal projections on a Hilbert space, N = ran P ∩ ran Q, P**_{N}** the orthogonal projection onto N, and c = ‖PQ − P**_{N}**‖, the cosine of the Friedrichs angle between ran P and ran Q. Then for every vector F,*

	 $‖(I − Q)PF‖ ≤ ‖(I − Q)F‖ + c ‖(I − P)F‖.$ 

*Proof. *By Halmos' theorem on two subspaces the space is the orthogonal sum of ran *P* ∩ ran *Q*, ran *P* ∩ ker *Q*, ker *P* ∩ ran *Q*, ker *P* ∩ ker *Q* and a generic part unitarily equivalent to *K* ⊕ *K*, on which *P* acts as the matrix with rows (*I*, 0), (0, 0) and *Q* as the matrix with rows (*C*², *CS*), (*CS*, *S*²), with *C* = cos Θ and *S* = sin Θ for a positive operator Θ on *K* with 0 < Θ < π/2 and trivial kernels of Θ and π/2 − Θ; every summand reduces *P* and *Q*. On the four trivial summands *PQ* − *P*_{N} vanishes, and on the generic part *P*_{N} = 0 and the norm of *PQ* is that of the row (*C*², *CS*), which is ‖*C*‖; so *c* = ‖*C*‖ when the generic part is not zero. On ran *P* ∩ ran *Q*, (*I* − *Q*)*PF* = 0; on ran *P* ∩ ker *Q*, (*I* − *Q*)*PF* = *F* = (*I* − *Q*)*F*; on the two summands inside ker *P*, *PF* = 0: on each the inequality holds with 0 in place of *c*. On the generic part write *F* = (*x*, *y*). Then *I* − *Q* is the matrix with rows (*S*², −*CS*), (−*CS*, *C*²), so (*I* − *Q*)*PF* = (*S*·*Sx*, −*C*·*Sx*), of norm ‖*Sx*‖ since *C*² + *S*² = *I*, and (*I* − *Q*)*F* = (*S*(*Sx* − *Cy*), −*C*(*Sx* − *Cy*)), of norm ‖*Sx* − *Cy*‖. Hence ‖(*I* − *Q*)*PF*‖ = ‖*Sx*‖ ≤ ‖*Sx* − *Cy*‖ + ‖*Cy*‖ ≤ ‖(*I* − *Q*)*F*‖ + *c*‖*y*‖, and ‖*y*‖ = ‖(*I* − *P*)*F*‖. The summands are orthogonal and preserved by *P* and *Q*, so Minkowski's inequality over them gives the claim. ∎[fn]

Lemma 8.5B (The angle between two coarse variables). *For distinct y, y′ the cosine c(y, y′) of the Friedrichs angle between H**_{y}** and H**_{y′}** is at most R**_{j}**(y, y′). Consequently, for every F ∈ L²(λ**_{j}**), ℓ**_{y}**(K**_{y}**F) = 0 and*

	 $ℓ_{y′}(K_{y}F) ≤ ℓ_{y′}(F) + R_{j}(y, y′) ℓ_{y}(F).$ 

*Proof. *The intersection *H*_{y} ∩ *H*_{y′} contains every function of ξ. The cosine *c*(*y*, *y*′) is the supremum of |⟨*F*, *G*⟩| over unit vectors *F* ∈ *H*_{y} orthogonal to *H*_{y} ∩ *H*_{y′} and *G* ∈ *H*_{y′} orthogonal to *H*_{y} ∩ *H*_{y′}. Such an *F* is a function of (*v*_{y′}, ξ) orthogonal to every function of ξ, so λ_{j}(*F* | ξ) = 0: for λ_{j}-almost every ξ, *F*(·, ξ) has mean zero under the law of *v*_{y′} under π_{ξ}. Likewise *G* is a function of (*v*_{y}, ξ) with *G*(·, ξ) of mean zero under the law of *v*_{y} under π_{ξ}. Disintegrating λ_{j} over ξ,

	 $⟨F, G⟩ = ∫ λ_{j}(dξ) ∫ π_{ξ}(dv_{y} dv_{y′}) F̄(v_{y′}, ξ) G(v_{y}, ξ),$ 

and the inner integral is at most ρ_{ξ}(*y*, *y*′) times the product of the norms of *F*(·, ξ) and *G*(·, ξ) in L²(π_{ξ}), by the definition of the maximal correlation. The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality over ξ gives |⟨*F*, *G*⟩| ≤ *R*_{j}(*y*, *y*′)‖*F*‖‖*G*‖, which is the first assertion. The second is ‖*Q*_{y}*K*_{y}*F*‖ = 0, and the third is Lemma 8.5A with *P* = *K*_{y} and *Q* = *K*_{y′}. ∎

Proposition 8.5C (The fluctuation vector decays). *Let L = Σ**_{y∈Y}** (K**_{y}** − I), a bounded self-adjoint operator on L²(λ**_{j}**), and P**_{t}** = e**^{tL}** the semigroup of the heat-bath dynamics that resamples each coarse variable from its conditional law at rate one. Write ℓ(F) = (ℓ**_{y}**(F))**_{y∈Y}**. Then for every F ∈ L²(λ**_{j}**) and every t ≥ 0, componentwise,*

	 $ℓ(P_{t}F) ≤ e^{−t(I − R_{j})} ℓ(F).$ 

*Proof. *For 0 < *h* ≤ 1/|*Y*|, *I* + *hL* = (1 − |*Y*|*h*)*I* + *h* Σ_{y} *K*_{y} with non-negative coefficients. Since ℓ_{z} is a seminorm, ℓ_{z}(*K*_{z}*G*) = 0, and ℓ_{z}(*K*_{y}*G*) ≤ ℓ_{z}(*G*) + *R*_{j}(*y*, *z*)ℓ_{y}(*G*) for *y* ≠ *z* by Lemma 8.5B,

	 $ℓ_{z}((I + hL)G) ≤ (1 − |Y|h)ℓ_{z}(G) + h(|Y| − 1)ℓ_{z}(G) + h Σ_{y≠z} R_{j}(y, z)ℓ_{y}(G) = (((1 − h)I + hR_{j})ℓ(G))_{z},$ 

the matrix *R*_{j} being symmetric. The matrix (1 − *h*)*I* + *hR*_{j} has non-negative entries, so it preserves the componentwise order, and induction gives ℓ((*I* + *tL*/*n*)^{n}*F*) ≤ ((1 − *t*/*n*)*I* + (*t*/*n*)*R*_{j})^{n} ℓ(*F*) for *n* ≥ *t*|*Y*|. As *n* → ∞, (*I* + *tL*/*n*)^{n} → *e*^{tL} in operator norm, *L* being bounded, each ℓ_{z} is continuous on L²(λ_{j}), and ((1 − *t*/*n*)*I* + (*t*/*n*)*R*_{j})^{n} → *e*^{−t(I−Rⱼ)}. ∎

Theorem 8.5D (The covariance theorem in L²; Edwin Boston III). *Suppose that ∩**_{y∈Y}** H**_{y}** consists of the constants and that the spectral radius ρ(R**_{j}**) of R**_{j}** is less than one. Then for all F and G in L²(λ**_{j}**),*

	 $|λ_{j}(FG) − λ_{j}(F)λ_{j}(G)| ≤ Σ_{x,y∈Y} ℓ_{x}(F) D(x, y) ℓ_{y}(G),     D = (I − R_{j})^{−1} = Σ_{n≥0} R_{j}^{n},$ 	(8.8c)

*with D entrywise non-negative, and*

	 $Var_{λ_{j}}(F) ≤ (1 − ρ(R_{j}))^{−1} Σ_{y∈Y} λ_{j}( Var_{λ_{j}}(F | v_{yᶜ}) ).$ 

*If moreover Σ**_{y′}** R**_{j}**(y, y′) e**^{κ|y−y′|}** ≤ α < 1 for every y, then ρ(R**_{j}**) ≤ α and D(x, y) ≤ e**^{−κ|x−y|}**/(1 − α). Under (S) the intersection ∩**_{y}** H**_{y}** consists of the constants.*

*Proof. *The Dirichlet form of the dynamics is *E*(*F*, *G*) = ⟨*F*, −*LG*⟩ = Σ_{y} ⟨*Q*_{y}*F*, *Q*_{y}*G*⟩, so |*E*(*F*, *G*)| ≤ Σ_{y} ℓ_{y}(*F*)ℓ_{y}(*G*). The kernel of *L* is ∩_{y} *H*_{y}, since *LF* = 0 gives Σ_{y} ‖*Q*_{y}*F*‖² = ⟨*F*, −*LF*⟩ = 0; it consists of the constants by assumption, so the spectral theorem for the bounded self-adjoint operator *L* ≤ 0 gives *P*_{t}*G* → λ_{j}(*G*) in L²(λ_{j}) as *t* → ∞. Since λ_{j}(*FG*) = ⟨*F̄*, *G*⟩ and (*d*/*dt*)⟨*F̄*, *P*_{t}*G*⟩ = −*E*(*F̄*, *P*_{t}*G*),

	 $λ_{j}(FG) − λ_{j}(F)λ_{j}(G) = ∫_{0}^{∞} E(F̄, P_{t}G) dt,$ 

and by Proposition 8.5C, with ℓ_{y}(*F̄*) = ℓ_{y}(*F*), |*E*(*F̄*, *P*_{t}*G*)| ≤ Σ_{y} ℓ_{y}(*F*)(*e*^{−t(I−Rⱼ)}ℓ(*G*))_{y}. The matrix *R*_{j} is symmetric with non-negative entries and spectral radius less than one, so *e*^{−t(I−Rⱼ)} = *e*^{−t}*e*^{tRⱼ} has non-negative entries and ∫₀^{∞} *e*^{−t(I−Rⱼ)} *dt* = (*I* − *R*_{j})⁻¹ = Σ_{n} *R*_{j}^{n}; this is (8.8c). For the variance, (*d*/*dt*)‖*P*_{t}*F* − λ_{j}(*F*)‖² = −2*E*(*P*_{t}*F*, *P*_{t}*F*) = −2|ℓ(*P*_{t}*F*)|², with |·| the euclidean norm on ℝ^{Y}; the componentwise domination of Proposition 8.5C by a non-negative vector gives |ℓ(*P*_{t}*F*)| ≤ |*e*^{−t(I−Rⱼ)}ℓ(*F*)| ≤ *e*^{−t(1−ρ(Rⱼ))}|ℓ(*F*)|, the largest eigenvalue of the symmetric non-negative *R*_{j} being its spectral radius, and integration over [0, ∞) gives Var(*F*) ≤ (1 − ρ(*R*_{j}))⁻¹|ℓ(*F*)|², which is the second inequality. Under the weighted row-sum bound put *R̃*(*y*, *y*′) = *R*_{j}(*y*, *y*′)*e*^{κ|y−y′|}, with row sums at most α; since |*y* − *y*″| ≤ |*y* − *y*′| + |*y*′ − *y*″|, induction on *n* gives *R*_{j}^{n}(*y*, *y*′)*e*^{κ|y−y′|} ≤ *R̃*^{n}(*y*, *y*′), and the row sums of *R̃*^{n} are at most α^{n}, so *D*(*x*, *y*)*e*^{κ|x−y|} ≤ Σ_{n} α^{n} = 1/(1 − α), while ρ(*R*_{j}) is at most the largest row sum of *R*_{j}, hence at most α. Under (S), λ_{j} and ∏*m* have the same null sets; a function in ∩_{y} *H*_{y} agrees ∏*m*-almost everywhere, for each *y*, with a function not depending on *v*_{y}, and integrating out the coordinates one at a time with Fubini's theorem on the product measure shows that it is ∏*m*-almost everywhere constant. ∎[fn]

Proposition 8.5E (The currency inequality). *With versions of the conditional laws λ**_{j}**(dv**_{y}** | v) defined for every coarse configuration, as in (8.5), for distinct y and y′*

	 $R_{j}(y, y′)^{2} ≤ C_{j}^{λ}(y, y′) C_{j}^{λ}(y′, y),     so that     R_{j}(y, y′) ≤ ½ ( C_{j}^{λ}(y, y′) + C_{j}^{λ}(y′, y) ).$ 	(8.8d)

*For a translation-covariant block map, Hypothesis I therefore implies Hypothesis I′ with the same κ and α. When V has two points, at λ**_{j}**-almost every environment ξ at which both coordinates charge both points, ρ**_{ξ}**(y, y′)² = C**_{ξ}**(y | y′) C**_{ξ}**(y′ | y) in the notation of the proof: the inequality of the proof cannot be improved.*

*Proof. *Fix *y* ≠ *y*′ and an environment ξ, and write *X* = *v*_{y}, *X*′ = *v*_{y′}. For λ_{j}-almost every ξ the single-site laws of (8.5) at the configurations (*X*, *X*′, ξ) are conditional laws of π_{ξ}, and the operator *M*,

	 $Mg(b) = ∫ λ_{j}(dv_{y} | X′ = b, ξ) ∫ λ_{j}(dv_{y′} | X = v_{y}, ξ) g(v_{y′}),$ 

which goes from *X*′ to *X* and back, is a Markov kernel defined at every point, self-adjoint on L²(π_{ξ}′), π_{ξ}′ the law of *X*′ under π_{ξ}, with norm ρ_{ξ}(*y*, *y*′)² on the functions of mean zero: it is *T*^{∗}*T* for the conditional expectation *T* from functions of *X*′ to functions of *X*, whose norm on the functions of mean zero is the maximal correlation. Its kernel is the composition of two kernels whose Dobrushin coefficients are *C*_{ξ}(*y* | *y*′) = sup_{b,b′} ‖λ_{j}(*dv*_{y} | *X*′ = *b*, ξ) − λ_{j}(*dv*_{y} | *X*′ = *b*′, ξ)‖_{TV} ≤ *C*^{λ}_{j}(*y*, *y*′) and *C*_{ξ}(*y*′ | *y*) ≤ *C*^{λ}_{j}(*y*′, *y*), and Dobrushin's coefficient is submultiplicative, so δ(*M*) ≤ *C*^{λ}_{j}(*y*, *y*′)*C*^{λ}_{j}(*y*′, *y*). It remains to show that the norm of *M* on the functions of mean zero is at most δ(*M*). For a bounded *g* of mean zero and *n* ≥ 1 the oscillation argument of Proposition 8.1 gives osc(*M*^{n}*g*) ≤ δ(*M*)^{n} osc(*g*), and *M*^{n}*g* has mean zero, so its L² norm is at most sup|*M*^{n}*g*| ≤ osc(*M*^{n}*g*). If the spectral projection *E* of the self-adjoint *M*, restricted to the functions of mean zero, onto {|λ| ≥ δ(*M*) + ε} were not zero for some ε > 0, there would be a bounded *g* of mean zero with *Eg* ≠ 0, the bounded functions being dense, and ‖*M*^{n}*g*‖ ≥ (δ(*M*) + ε)^{n}‖*Eg*‖ would contradict ‖*M*^{n}*g*‖ ≤ δ(*M*)^{n} osc(*g*) for large *n*. Hence ρ_{ξ}(*y*, *y*′)² ≤ *C*^{λ}_{j}(*y*, *y*′)*C*^{λ}_{j}(*y*′, *y*) for λ_{j}-almost every ξ, which is the first assertion, and the arithmetic–geometric mean inequality gives the second. For a translation-covariant block map the blocked measure is invariant under the translations of *Y*, so *C*^{λ}_{j}(*y*, *y*′) depends on *y*′ − *y* alone and the weighted column sums of *C*^{λ}_{j} equal its weighted row sums; then Σ_{y′} *R*_{j}(*y*, *y*′)*e*^{κ|y−y′|} ≤ ½ Σ_{y′} (*C*^{λ}_{j}(*y*, *y*′) + *C*^{λ}_{j}(*y*′, *y*))*e*^{κ|y−y′|} ≤ α. When *V* = {0, 1}, write *p*_{ab} for the probability of *X* = *a*, *X*′ = *b* under π_{ξ}, *p*_{a·} and *p*_{·b} for its marginals, and Δ = *p*₁₁*p*₀₀ − *p*₁₀*p*₀₁; every function of a two-point variable is affine in its indicator, so ρ_{ξ} = |Δ|/(*p*_{1·}*p*_{0·}*p*_{·1}*p*_{·0})^{1/2}, while *C*_{ξ}(*y* | *y*′) = |*p*₁₁/*p*_{·1} − *p*₁₀/*p*_{·0}| = |Δ|/(*p*_{·1}*p*_{·0}) and *C*_{ξ}(*y*′ | *y*) = |Δ|/(*p*_{1·}*p*_{0·}), whose product is ρ_{ξ}². ∎

Theorem 8.5′ (The reduction of the gap to the blocked bound, in L²). *Let a construction be blocked at the coarse scale a**_{c}** as in Theorem 8.5, with a block map whose blocked measures have the form (S), and suppose Hypotheses I′ and II′. Put ρ = max(e**^{−κ}**, ρ′). Then for every ρ₁ with ρ < ρ₁ < 1 there is a constant K, depending on ρ, ρ₁, α, K′, N(f), N(g), ‖f‖ and ‖g‖ and on nothing else, such that at every rung j ≥ j₀, on every lattice of the construction, for all bounded observables f and g, |S**_{j}**(fg) − S**_{j}**(f) S**_{j}**(g)| ≤ K ρ₁**^{d}**, with d the distance between S(f) and S(g); and every limit point of the construction's Schwinger functions, on the sector generated by the observables that are bounded uniformly in j, has a unique vacuum and a Hamiltonian with no spectrum in (0, m), with m = (1/a**_{c}**) min(κ, log(1/ρ′)), the number of (8.8).*

*Proof. *Fix *j* ≥ *j*₀, a lattice, and bounded observables *f* and *g*; write *F*(*v*) = ν_{j}(*f* | *v*) and *G*(*v*) = ν_{j}(*g* | *v*) for their fibre means, bounded by ‖*f*‖ and ‖*g*‖. The covariance splits along the block map, by the law of total covariance:

	 $μ_{j}(fg) − μ_{j}(f)μ_{j}(g) = [λ_{j}(FG) − λ_{j}(F)λ_{j}(G)] + λ_{j}( ν_{j}(fg | ·) − FG ).$ 

The second term is at most *K*′*N*(*f*)‖*f*‖‖*g*‖ρ′^{d} by Hypothesis II′(a). For the first, Hypothesis I′ gives ρ(*R*_{j}) ≤ α < 1 and *D*(*i*, *k*) ≤ *e*^{−κ|i−k|}/(1 − α), (S) identifies ∩_{y} *H*_{y} with the constants, and Theorem 8.5D gives |λ_{j}(*FG*) − λ_{j}(*F*)λ_{j}(*G*)| ≤ Σ_{i,k} ℓ_{i}(*F*)*D*(*i*, *k*)ℓ_{k}(*G*). Proposition 8.4′ (The tilt lemma in L²; Edwin Boston III). *Let π be the law of a pair (X, W) of random variables with values in compact metric spaces, δ a bounded measurable function of (x, w), and π′ = e**^{δ}**π/π(e**^{δ}**) the tilt of π by δ. Put o**_{X}** = sup**_{w}** osc**_{x}** δ(x, w), o**_{W}** = sup**_{x}** osc**_{w}** δ(x, w), and Δ = sup**_{w,w′}** osc**_{x}** (δ(x, w) − δ(x, w′)), the mixed second difference of δ in the sense of Proposition 8.4. Then the maximal correlations of the two laws satisfy*

	 $ρ(π′) ≤ e^{(o_{X} + o_{W})/2} ρ(π) + 1 − e^{−2Δ}.$ 	(8.8e)

*Proof. *If (*X*, *W*) is almost surely constant under π, both maximal correlations vanish and there is nothing to prove; otherwise three facts. First, for every law π of (*X*, *W*) under which (*X*, *W*) is not almost surely constant,

	 $1 − ρ(π) = inf { [ π(Var_{π}(F | X)) + π(Var_{π}(F | W)) ] / Var_{π}(F) : F ∈ L²(π) not constant }.$ 

Let *P*_{X} and *P*_{W} be the conditional expectations onto the functions of *X* and of *W*, orthogonal projections on L²(π), so that the numerator is ‖*F* − *P*_{X}*F*‖² + ‖*F* − *P*_{W}*F*‖² = ⟨*F*, (2*I* − *P*_{X} − *P*_{W})*F*⟩. If ρ(π) < 1, the ranges of *P*_{X} and *P*_{W} meet in the constants, a nonconstant function of *X* almost surely equal to a function of *W* having correlation one with it, and the cosine of the Friedrichs angle between them is ρ(π); in the decomposition of Lemma 8.5A, *P*_{X} + *P*_{W} has spectrum {1 ± cos θ} on the generic part, 1 on the two mixed summands and 0 on ker *P*_{X} ∩ ker *P*_{W}, and since *X* or *W* is not almost surely constant, the generic part or a mixed summand is not zero; so the spectrum of *P*_{X} + *P*_{W} off the constants has supremum 1 + ρ(π), and the infimum above is 1 − ρ(π). If ρ(π) = 1, either the two ranges share a nonconstant function, at which the quotient vanishes, or the cosine is one and the same decomposition makes the infimum zero.

Second, if π″ = *h*π with *a* ≤ *h* ≤ *b* for constants 0 < *a* ≤ *b*, then 1 − ρ(π″) ≥ (*a*/*b*)(1 − ρ(π)): indeed π″(Var_{π″}(*F* | *X*)) = inf_{c} π″((*F* − *c*(*X*))²) ≥ *a* inf_{c} π((*F* − *c*(*X*))²) = *a* π(Var_{π}(*F* | *X*)), the infimum over functions *c* of *X*, the same holds with *W*, and Var_{π″}(*F*) = inf_{c} π″((*F* − *c*)²) ≤ *b* Var_{π}(*F*); the first fact finishes.

Third, if π₁ = (*u* ⊗ *s*)π/π(*u* ⊗ *s*) for bounded functions *u* of *x* and *s* of *w* with positive infima, then ρ(π₁) ≤ (sup *u* · sup *s*/(inf *u* · inf *s*))^{1/2} ρ(π). Write β(*x*) = π(*s*(*W*) | *X* = *x*), ζ(*w*) = π(*u*(*X*) | *W* = *w*) and *Z* = π(*u* ⊗ *s*); the laws of *X* and *W* under π₁ have densities *u*β/*Z* and *s*ζ/*Z* with respect to those under π, so the maps *f* ↦ *f*(*u*β/*Z*)^{1/2} and *g* ↦ *g*(*s*ζ/*Z*)^{1/2} are unitary from L²(π₁^{X}) onto L²(π^{X}) and from L²(π₁^{W}) onto L²(π^{W}), and π₁(*f*(*X*)*g*(*W*)) = π(*a*₁*f̃*(*X*) · *a*₂*g̃*(*W*)) with *f̃*, *g̃* the images and *a*₁ = (*u*/β)^{1/2}, *a*₂ = (*s*/ζ)^{1/2}. Hence the operator *T*₁ of π₁, *T*₁*g* = π₁(*g*(*W*) | *X*), is unitarily equivalent to *a*₁*Ta*₂, *T* the operator of π and *a*₁, *a*₂ acting by multiplication. For each of these laws the operator maps the constants to the constants and the functions of mean zero to the functions of mean zero, so it is the orthogonal sum of the identity on the constants and its restriction to mean zero, whose norm is the maximal correlation; the maximal correlation is therefore the second singular number inf{‖*A* − *E*‖ : rank *E* ≤ 1} of the operator. Writing *T* = 1 ⊗ 1 + *T*₀ with ‖*T*₀‖ = ρ(π), the operator *a*₁*Ta*₂ is a rank-one operator plus *a*₁*T*₀*a*₂, so its second singular number, which is ρ(π₁), is at most ‖*a*₁‖_{∞}‖*a*₂‖_{∞}ρ(π); and β ≥ inf *s*, ζ ≥ inf *u* give ‖*a*₁‖_{∞}‖*a*₂‖_{∞} ≤ (sup *u* · sup *s*/(inf *u* · inf *s*))^{1/2}.

Now fix (*x*₀, *w*₀) and write δ(*x*, *w*) = δ(*x*, *w*₀) + (δ(*x*₀, *w*) − δ(*x*₀, *w*₀)) + δ₁₂(*x*, *w*), where δ₁₂(*x*, *w*) = (δ(*x*, *w*) − δ(*x*, *w*₀)) − (δ(*x*₀, *w*) − δ(*x*₀, *w*₀)) is at most Δ in modulus. The tilt by the first two terms is a tilt by *u* ⊗ *s* with sup *u*/inf *u* ≤ exp(*o*_{X}) and sup *s*/inf *s* ≤ exp(*o*_{W}), so the third fact gives ρ(π₁) ≤ exp((*o*_{X} + *o*_{W})/2) ρ(π); the tilt of π₁ by δ₁₂ has a density between two constants of ratio at most *e*^{2Δ}, so the second fact gives 1 − ρ(π′) ≥ *e*^{−2Δ}(1 − ρ(π₁)). Hence ρ(π′) ≤ *e*^{−2Δ}ρ(π₁) + 1 − *e*^{−2Δ} ≤ exp((*o*_{X} + *o*_{W})/2) ρ(π) + 1 − *e*^{−2Δ}. ∎[fn]

The oscillations along each variable enter (8.8e) as a factor and the mixed difference as an addend, as in (8.7); an addend carrying the oscillations would not vanish for a pair on which δ has no joint term, while 1 − *e*^{−2Δ} does, and it is the addends that the weighted row sums of Corollary 8.5F add up.

Corollary 8.5F (The effective action and the fourth link, in L²). *(i) When the effective action A**_{j}** of (S) is bounded and the conditional laws λ**_{j}**(dv**_{y}** | v) ∝ exp(−A**_{j}**(v)) m(dv**_{y}**) are used in (8.5), R**_{j}**(y, y′) ≤ 1 − exp(−Δ**_{yy′}**(A**_{j}**)) for distinct y and y′, with Δ**_{yy′}**(A**_{j}**) the mixed second difference of the effective action in the sense of Proposition 8.4.*

*(ii) Let the blocked measures of rungs j ≥ j₁ on a common coarse torus be tilts λ**_{j}** = e**^{δⱼ}**λ**_{j₁}**/λ**_{j₁}**(e**^{δⱼ}**) of that of the rung j₁ by bounded functions δ**_{j}**. Then*

	 $R_{j}(y, y′) ≤ e^{(o_{y}(δ_{j}) + o_{y′}(δ_{j}))/2} R_{j₁}(y, y′) + 1 − e^{−2Δ_{yy′}(δ_{j})},$ 

*with o**_{y}** and Δ**_{yy′}** as in Proposition 8.4; consequently, if o**_{j}** = sup**_{y}** o**_{y}**(δ**_{j}**) and sup**_{y}** Σ**_{y′}**(1 − exp(−2Δ**_{yy′}**(δ**_{j}**))) e**^{κ|y−y′|}** tend to zero, Hypothesis I′ at the rung j₁ with the constant α₁ implies it at every sufficiently late rung with any α ∈ (α₁, 1).*

*Proof. *(i) The conditional laws λ_{j}(*dv*_{y} | *v*) ∝ exp(−*A*_{j}(*v*))*m*(*dv*_{y}) are defined for every *v* when *A*_{j} is bounded, the normalisations lying between exp(−sup *A*_{j}) and exp(−inf *A*_{j}), and (8.5) and Proposition 8.5E are read with them. The conditional law of *v*_{y} under one of two coarse configurations differing only at *y*′ is the tilt of its conditional law under the other by the difference of the two actions, so the first inequality of Proposition 8.4 gives *C*^{λ}_{j}(*y*, *y*′) ≤ 1 − exp(−Δ_{yy′}(*A*_{j})). The mixed difference is the supremum of |*A*_{j}(*a*, *b*) − *A*_{j}(*a*, *b*′) − *A*_{j}(*a*′, *b*) + *A*_{j}(*a*′, *b*′)| over the values *a*, *a*′ of *v*_{y}, *b*, *b*′ of *v*_{y′} and the environment, so Δ_{yy′}(*A*_{j}) = Δ_{y′y}(*A*_{j}), and Proposition 8.5E gives *R*_{j}(*y*, *y*′) ≤ (*C*^{λ}_{j}(*y*, *y*′)*C*^{λ}_{j}(*y*′, *y*))^{1/2} ≤ 1 − exp(−Δ_{yy′}(*A*_{j})).

(ii) The measures λ_{j} and λ_{j₁} are equivalent, with density proportional to *e*^{δⱼ}, so for λ_{j}-almost every environment ξ the conditional law of (*v*_{y}, *v*_{y′}) under λ_{j} is the tilt of that under λ_{j₁} by δ_{j}(·, ·, ξ), whose oscillations along *v*_{y} and *v*_{y′} are at most *o*_{y}(δ_{j}) and *o*_{y′}(δ_{j}) and whose mixed difference is at most Δ_{yy′}(δ_{j}); Proposition 8.4′ and the essential supremum over ξ give the bound. Weighting and summing, Σ_{y′} *R*_{j}(*y*, *y*′)*e*^{κ|y−y′|} ≤ *e*^{oⱼ}α₁ + Σ_{y′}(1 − exp(−2Δ_{yy′}(δ_{j})))*e*^{κ|y−y′|}, which is below any α ∈ (α₁, 1) once the two tails are small. ∎

Corollary 8.5F (ii) is the fourth link of §8.1 in the L² currency, with the inputs that (8.7) asks of the tilts in total variation: their oscillations and their weighted mixed differences tending to zero.
